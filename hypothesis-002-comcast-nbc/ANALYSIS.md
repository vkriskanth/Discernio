# Analysis: Comcast / NBCUniversal Separation

*Prepared 2026-07-12. All market data pulled live via `market_data.py` (prices through
2026-07-10), financials from SEC EDGAR via `sec_financials.py`, and deal math in
`deal_math.py`. Sources linked at the bottom.*

## First, correcting the premises

The hypothesis is directionally right but the facts need tightening:

1. **"Comcast decides to separate NBC unit" — TRUE, and it happened in two stages.**
   Stage 1: the cable networks (USA, CNBC, MSNBC→"MS NOW", Syfy, E!, Oxygen, Golf
   Channel, plus Fandango/Rotten Tomatoes) were spun off as **Versant Media Group
   (VSNT)**, completed **January 2, 2026** (1 VSNT share per 25 CMCSA). Stage 2: on
   **June 29, 2026** Comcast announced a tax-free spin of **all of NBCUniversal plus
   Sky** (NBC, Telemundo, Bravo, Peacock, Universal Pictures, theme parks), targeted
   for mid-2027. Mike Cavanagh becomes NBCU CEO; Michael Angelakis becomes Comcast CEO.
2. **"Shares down 30% over past year" — close.** CMCSA is **-25.6% over 1 year,
   -49% over 5 years**, and -52% from its 5-year high. It trades at **4.6x trailing
   earnings** with a 5.7% dividend yield — deep-value/distressed-conglomerate pricing.
3. **"YouTube is a behemoth" — TRUE.** Alphabet is +102% over 1 year, $4.36T market cap.
4. **"Streaming led by Netflix is profitable" — true for Netflix only.** Netflix earns
   real profits ($308.9B cap, ~24x earnings — even after a 41% one-year drawdown).
   Peacock lost money all of FY2025 (~$2.2B EBITDA loss, improving ~$700M y/y).
5. **"NBC has Fast & Furious, Law & Order" — right IP, right entity.** Fast & Furious
   is Universal Pictures and Law & Order is Universal Television/NBC — both go with
   the NBCU spinco (not Versant), along with Jurassic, Minions/Despicable Me, Wicked.

Read: the market pays 20-27x EBITDA for streaming/platform winners, ~11-13x for
scaled content, and 3-5x for anything attached to linear TV or cable pipes. Comcast,
a conglomerate of all three, gets priced at the pipe multiple. That gap **is** the
spinoff thesis.

## Objective 1 — Why did they buy it, at what price, is separating now right, do they lose revenue?

**Why they bought it (2009-2013):** GE was a distressed seller after the financial
crisis and Comcast wanted a hedge against cable-video decline — vertical integration
of distribution (cable) with content (NBC, Universal, parks). They bought
counter-cyclically and cheap.

**Price:** **~$31.9B total** — $13.8B for 51% (closed Jan 2011), $16.7B for GE's
remaining 49% (Mar 2013), plus $1.4B for the 30 Rock/CNBC real estate.

**Was it a good deal? Yes — one of the best media acquisitions of its era.**
Content & Experiences produced **$6.5B of Adjusted EBITDA in FY2025 alone — a ~20%
annual EBITDA yield on the original cost** (FY2025 10-K). Since the purchase, Comcast
built Epic Universe (parks did >$1.0B EBITDA in a single quarter for the first time
in Q4 2025), launched Peacock to 44M+ subs, and produced billion-dollar film
franchises (Wicked: $1.3B+ worldwide). Compare: GE sold at roughly 8x NBCU's
then-EBITDA; the assets earned that back several times over.

**Is separating now the best option?** A spin, not a sale — so the right question is
"does separation create value?" The evidence says the market thinks so: CMCSA rose
**+6.0% on announcement day** ($22.86 → $24.22) and holds +3.1% since. Our
sum-of-parts on disclosed FY2025 EBITDA: Theme Parks $3.1B × 9-12x + Studios $1.1B ×
9-11x + Media-ex-Versant ~$1.0B × 5-7x ≈ **$42-56B enterprise value before even
counting Sky** — versus $84B for the entire company today. The conglomerate discount
is real, and a tax-free spin is the cheapest way to attack it (a cash sale of a
~$50B+ appreciated asset would trigger enormous tax).

**Do they lose revenue?** Mechanically yes: Content & Experiences was $45.6B of
$123.7B FY2025 revenue (~37%). But shareholders keep it via spinco shares — nothing
is "lost," it's re-addressed. RemainCo keeps the better P&L: $80.9B of connectivity
revenue at **40% EBITDA margins** ($32.4B). Real dis-synergies exist but are modest:
Peacock/Xfinity bundling, cross-promotion of NBCU content on the platform, shared ad
sales, and Olympics-scale marketing leverage.

## Objective 2 — Who is going to buy this?

**Nobody, initially — that's the design.** It's a tax-free spin to existing
shareholders. Brian Roberts said the split is "absolutely not" a step toward a sale,
and IRS Section 355 rules effectively penalize a change of control for ~2 years
post-spin. The Roberts family's supervoting stock is a further deterrent.

But the structure unambiguously puts NBCU "in play" after the window closes
(~2029), and the 2025-26 land grab shows who shops in this aisle:

- **Paramount Skydance (Ellison)** just won Warner Bros. Discovery for **$110.9B
  ($31/share cash, Feb 2026)** after a bidding war. They'll be digesting for years —
  probably out.
- **Netflix** bid ~$72B for Warner's studio+streaming and **walked away** rather than
  overpay. A focused NBCU spinco (Universal's film IP + Peacock, without cable pipes)
  at a $40-60B class valuation is a cleaner, cheaper second bite.
- **Comcast itself** bid for WBD assets and lost — evidence management wanted more
  content scale, and the spinco (with its own stock as currency) can consolidate in
  ways Comcast-attached NBCU politically and financially couldn't.
- **Apple/Amazon** remain the wildcards with the balance sheets; sports rights and
  franchise IP fit, though NBC's broadcast licenses and the parks don't.
- **Versant**, meanwhile, is itself a roll-up vehicle for orphaned cable networks
  (A+E, AMC, Starz-type assets) rather than a target of size.

## Objective 3 — Comcast's revenue streams; any play against YouTube and Netflix?

FY2025 segments (10-K, $M):

| Segment | Revenue | Adj. EBITDA | Margin | Post-split home |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Residential Connectivity & Platforms | 70,704 | 26,653 | 37.7% | **Comcast** (RemainCo) |
| Business Services Connectivity | 10,237 | 5,725 | 55.9% | **Comcast** (RemainCo) |
| Media (incl. Versant nets thru 1/2/26) | 27,090 | 3,196 | 11.8% | NBCU spinco / Versant |
| Studios | 11,286 | 1,099 | 9.7% | NBCU spinco |
| Theme Parks | 9,836 | 3,080 | 31.3% | NBCU spinco |

**The streaming play is Peacock, and it is real but subscale.** 46M paid subs as of
Q1 2026 (vs 41M a year ago), FY2025 revenue $5.4B (+10%), Q1 2026 revenue $2.0B
(+67% y/y on Winter Olympics + NBA), losses narrowing fast (-$432M in Q1 2026;
management guided Peacock to "approach profitability" in Q2 2026). Its differentiation
is **live sports**: Sunday Night Football (primetime's #1 show, 15 straight years),
the NBA (returned to NBC in the 2025-26 season), Olympics, Premier League. Combined
with Sky's ~tens of millions of European relationships, the spinco is a legitimate
global #4-5 streamer.

But be honest about scale: Netflix does ~$47B TTM revenue at real margins; YouTube
rides Alphabet's $4.4T platform and pays nothing for most content. Peacock at $6-8B
run-rate revenue cannot win a content-spend war; it can win the "live + franchise IP"
niche. And note the sharpest fact: **post-split, Comcast RemainCo has *no* play
against YouTube/Netflix at all — by choice.** It becomes the toll road (broadband +
wireless at 40% margins) that carries them. That is the whole point of the split:
stop letting the market punish the pipes for owning a subscale streamer.

## Objective 4 — Is this advantageous to Comcast in the longer run?

**Modestly yes — through focus and optionality, not magic.** The bull case, with data:

- The conglomerate discount is measurable: 4.8x EV/EBITDA for the whole against
  parts that comp at 9-12x (parks, studios). Evercore's post-spin target implies
  ~$36 vs $23.57 today.
- RemainCo (broadband/wireless/business services) becomes the natural merger partner
  for **Charter** — cable consolidation is the endgame for a maturing pipe business,
  and it couldn't happen with NBCU attached.
- NBCU spinco gets its own currency for content M&A right as the industry consolidates
  (WBD gone to Paramount; A+E and others orphaned).

The honest bear case:

- **Spins of melting assets can just melt publicly**: Versant is **-21% since its
  December 2025 when-issued debut** — the market didn't re-rate it, it re-priced it.
- The crown jewel has its own problem: broadband is under fixed-wireless/fiber attack
  (Charter -67% in a year is the cable bear case in one number). The split does
  nothing to fix competition; it only clarifies it.
- Dis-synergies and duplicate public-company costs are real, and NBCU will carry a
  slug of the debt.

Net: separation raises the probability that both halves are sensibly priced and
sensibly run, and history (GE's own breakup, AT&T shedding WarnerMedia) says focused
successors outperform the conglomerate's counterfactual. But structure isn't
strategy — the FY2025 numbers say the value was never destroyed by NBCU (20% yield
on cost); it was obscured by what the market pays for cable pipes.

## Objective 5 — Who benefits most from buying NBC assets?

Ranked, as an analyst's judgment on the data above:

1. **Comcast shareholders** — the only party guaranteed to "get" the assets, tax-free,
   at a conglomerate-discount cost basis. If the spinco merely re-rates to Disney's
   ~11x EV/EBITDA, that's the cheapest value transfer in this whole story.
2. **Netflix** (post-2029, or for pieces) — it needs exactly what NBCU has (deep
   franchise film IP: Fast & Furious, Jurassic, Minions, Wicked; live sports rights
   infrastructure) and just demonstrated both the appetite (bid ~$72B for Warner) and
   the discipline (walked at $31/share). A $40-60B-class NBCU is the cheaper, cleaner
   version of the deal it wanted.
3. **Charter** — benefits on the *other* side: merging with a media-free Comcast
   RemainCo is the largest synergy pool in US cable.
4. **Paramount Skydance** — only if regulators forced WBD asset sales that pair with
   NBCU pieces; otherwise it's fully occupied.
5. **Versant** — smallest absolute winner, but as the designated consolidator of
   declining cable networks it gets to buy peers at 3x EBITDA with nobody else bidding.

## Bottom line

The hypothesis survives contact with the data. Comcast bought NBCU from a distressed
GE for ~$31.9B, earned roughly its purchase price back several times in EBITDA, and
is now spinning it — first Versant (done, Jan 2026), then all of NBCU+Sky (mid-2027) —
because the market pays 4-5x for conglomerated cable-plus-media but 9-12x for the
media parts alone. Nobody "buys" NBC in this transaction; shareholders receive it.
The most probable eventual buyer of NBCU assets is Netflix, and the most probable
beneficiary of the whole exercise is a Comcast shareholder who holds both pieces —
provided broadband holds up, which is the real risk nobody's spinning away.
