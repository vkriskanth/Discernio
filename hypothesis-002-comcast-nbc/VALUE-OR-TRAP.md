# Follow-up: Why Is Comcast Still Down After the Spinoff News? Value Play or Value Trap?

*Prepared 2026-07-12, as a follow-up to [ANALYSIS.md](ANALYSIS.md). Prices through
2026-07-10 from `data/prices.csv` (pulled via `market_data.py`); recent news and
analyst views from web sources linked at the bottom.*

## First, define "still down" precisely

| Measure | Value |
|---|---:|
| Close before announcement (2026-06-26) | $22.86 |
| Announcement-week high close (2026-06-30) | $24.22 |
| Latest close (2026-07-10) | $23.57 |
| Change since pre-announcement | **+3.1%** |
| Change from the post-announcement high | **-2.7%** |
| S&P 500 over the same window | **+3.6%** |
| YTD 2026 | -12.7% |
| vs 52-week high ($31.99) | -26% |

So the precise answer to "why is it still down": **the announcement pop has fully
faded on a market-relative basis.** CMCSA is up 3.1% since the day before the news
while the S&P is up 3.6% — the market has awarded the spinoff roughly **zero net
credit** after two weeks of reflection. That is a deliberate verdict, not neglect.
Peers tell the same story: Disney -2.5%, Charter -2.2%, Versant -1.0% over the same
window — the whole cable/media complex kept sinking and Comcast merely sank with it
from a slightly higher starting point.

## Why the market won't re-rate it (yet) — six reasons, in order of weight

### 1. The spinoff fixes the *smaller* problem

The sum-of-parts math in ANALYSIS.md values the NBCU spinco at ~$42-56B EV against
an $84B market cap for the whole company. But that means **most of what you own is
still RemainCo: cable broadband** — and broadband is the thing the market is actually
repricing. Q1 2026 revenue missed estimates on continued broadband subscriber losses;
T-Mobile and Verizon fixed-wireless keep taking share; and Comcast's competitive
response (simplified pricing, **no rate hikes in 2026**, free wireless lines) defends
subscribers by *sacrificing ARPU*. Charter — the pure-play version of RemainCo — is
-67% over one year. The spin separates the melting ice cube from the appreciating
asset, but you keep both cubes.

### 2. The value arrives in mid-2027, and the market doesn't prepay for execution

The NBCU+Sky spin closes ~12 months out. Until then there is no Form 10, no debt
allocation between the two companies, no standalone financials, no index treatment,
no dividend policy for either side. Event-driven funds typically size up *after*
those documents exist. Deutsche Bank's take captures the street view: separation
"provides flexibility but does not guarantee immediate transactions."

### 3. Versant is the live counterexample, trading on the same screen

The last time Comcast told shareholders "the parts are worth more separately,"
it spun Versant — which is **-21% since its debut**. The market didn't re-rate
the cable networks; it re-priced them. Anyone underwriting the 2027 spin has to
price the risk that the NBCU spinco (still ~40% linear-TV revenue, Peacock not yet
profitable) gets the Versant treatment rather than the Disney multiple.

### 4. Forced and fatigued sellers

Post-split, RemainCo is a pure telecom and the spinco a pure media company — many
current holders (dividend funds, telecom mandates) will be structural sellers of one
half or the other, and index funds mechanically sell spincos below index thresholds.
The market front-runs known future supply. Add five years of drawdown (-49%) and
there is no natural incremental buyer until the numbers force the issue.

### 5. Governance and regulatory frictions

Analysts flagged that the NBCU spinco will carry a **dual-class structure**
(Roberts family supervoting), which typically earns a governance discount and
deters the eventual acquirer the bull case depends on. Sky's news operations may
face UK regulatory scrutiny in a separation. And the endgame merger that makes
RemainCo exciting — Comcast-Charter — faces the DOJ history Craig Moffett points
out (the DOJ was prepared to block Comcast-Time Warner Cable) plus state-by-state
approvals and a combined debt load.

### 6. "Cheap" is only cheap if E holds

4.6x trailing earnings and a 5.7% yield is either a coiled spring or a value trap,
and the difference is one variable: **the slope of broadband EBITDA**. Charter at
5.3x, Versant at 3.1x, and PSKY's legacy assets show the market happily leaves
declining-cash-flow businesses at 3-5x forever. The multiple is not the debate;
the denominator is.

## So is it a value play everyone is missing?

**Nobody is "missing" it — the discount is the market's compensation for a real,
unresolved question.** That said, the asymmetry is genuinely interesting:

**What you're paid to wait:** a 5.7% dividend yield, a 4.6x P/E, and $32.4B of
connectivity EBITDA at 40% margins backing it.

**What you get for free at this price:** essentially the entire re-rating option.
Sum-of-parts says NBCU spinco alone could be worth half today's EV; Evercore's
post-spin work implies ~$36 vs $23.57 (+53%); street consensus targets sit around
$40-43 even from Hold-rated analysts.

**Real catalysts with dates:**
- **Q2 2026 earnings, July 23** — the near-term one. Two swing factors: broadband
  net losses (Q1 improved >100k y/y — a second straight improvement would attack
  reason #1 directly) and Peacock, which management guided to "approach
  profitability" this quarter.
- **Form 10 filing (late 2026/early 2027)** — debt allocation and standalone
  margins; this is when event funds can actually underwrite it.
- **Project Genesis completion (2027)** — network upgrade done, capex rolls off,
  RemainCo free cash flow inflects.
- **Spin completion (mid-2027)** — two clean stories; Charter-merger optionality
  becomes discussable.

**What would make it a trap instead:** broadband losses re-accelerating or ARPU
declines steepening (watch the July 23 print), Peacock missing its profitability
guide, or a Versant-style reception telegraphed by weak when-issued trading in 2027.

## Bottom line

Comcast is "still down" because the spinoff announcement changed *how the assets
are packaged*, not *what the biggest asset earns* — and the biggest asset, broadband,
is the one under attack. The market's refusal to re-rate is rational patience, not
an oversight: it is waiting for evidence (broadband trajectory, Form 10 economics,
Peacock profitability) that won't fully arrive until mid-2027. That makes CMCSA a
**legitimate but slow value play with a measurable trap-door**: you collect ~6% to
wait, the re-rating option is nearly free, and the thesis is falsifiable on specific
dates — starting July 23. It is mispriced only if broadband merely *erodes* rather
than *melts*; that distinction, not the spinoff, is the whole trade.
