# ICLR — Momentum Note

**Thesis**

ICLR's tape is a re-acceleration story, not a mature uptrend: the 12-month
return is a muted +4.9% while the last three months alone delivered +37.4%,
meaning the name was dead money (or worse) for most of the year before
inflecting sharply. RSI14 at 47.6 is neutral despite that surge, and price is
only 13.7% above the 200DMA — both say the move isn't extended or overbought,
leaving room to run rather than screaming for mean reversion. Positioning
argues this isn't short-covering fuel: float short interest is thin at 3.3%
with just 2.7 days to cover, so the rally is being driven by fresh buying, not
a squeeze. What stands out is who's doing the buying — Glenn Greenberg
(Brave Warrior) opened a new position sized at a striking 8.2% of portfolio,
Nygren (Oakmark) added 73% to his stake, and Greenhaven added 55%, all
concentrated value investors making large, high-conviction commitments in
Q1 2026 into the same window the stock started re-rating, which reads as
smart money front-running (or confirming) a fundamental inflection — forward
PE of 13.8 versus a trailing PE of 252.9 implies the market expects an
earnings recovery that hasn't shown up in revenue yet (growth is just 1.2%).
The one discordant note is the options tape: a put/call OI ratio of 1.93 is
unusually put-heavy for a stock with this much price strength, suggesting
either protective hedging by holders locking in gains or genuine skepticism
that the fundamental gap will close.

**Score adjustment**

adjusted_score: 61 — shade up from 55.3 because the composite's
`short_squeeze` (16.3) and `options_skew` (0.0) sub-scores understate a
momentum setup that's non-overbought (RSI 47.6), still only modestly extended
vs. the 200DMA, and now has multiple concentrated value superinvestors
putting real portfolio weight behind it right at the inflection; the elevated
put/call skew keeps the adjustment modest rather than a stronger upgrade.

**Key risk**

The rally is running well ahead of the fundamentals — revenue growth is
still just 1.2% and ROE is near zero, so the forward-PE re-rating is pricing
a CRO demand recovery (biotech funding, clinical trial volume) that hasn't
materialized in the top line yet. If that inflection doesn't show up in the
next couple of prints, the stock has no earnings support under the last
three months of gains, and the put-heavy options positioning suggests part
of the market is already hedged for exactly that disappointment.
