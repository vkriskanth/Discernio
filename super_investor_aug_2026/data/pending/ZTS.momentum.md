## ZTS — Momentum Note

### Thesis

ZTS is a broken long-term trend trying to find a floor: down 49.5% over 12 months and still 32.4% below its 200DMA, but the rate of decay has collapsed from -42.0% over six months to just -2.5% over the last one, with RSI14 sitting at a neutral 40.8 rather than an oversold extreme — this looks like exhaustion, not a fresh leg down. Positioning offers no tailwind: short interest is thin at 5.06% of float with only 2.75 days to cover, so there's no squeeze fuel behind any bounce, and the 1.76x put/call open-interest ratio shows options traders are still paying up for downside protection rather than betting on a turn. Five value-oriented superinvestors (Ruane Cunniff, Tweedy Browne, Wedgewood Partners, Mairs & Power, Markel/Gayner) built or added to positions across Q1-Q2 2026, with Ruane Cunniff's 4.76%-of-portfolio new stake and Tweedy Browne's 3.03% new stake standing out as high-conviction entries — but both cohorts bought into a decline that kept going after their filing quarters (the 6-month and 3-month returns are still deeply negative from those windows), so this reads as classic early value accumulation into a falling knife rather than confirmation that price momentum has turned. Beta of 0.73 confirms the drawdown is idiosyncratic, not macro-driven, so any reversal needs a company-specific catalyst rather than a market-wide risk-on rotation to bail it out. Net: the deceleration argues the worst of the selling may be behind it, but nothing in positioning or options confirms a turn yet, and the value buying is a lagging, not leading, indicator here.

### Key risk

The apparent deceleration (-2.5% 1M vs. -42% 6M) could be a pause rather than a turn: RSI14 at 40.8 hasn't even cleared into bullish territory, price is still 32% under the 200DMA, and revenue growth is flat-to-negative (-0.2%), so if the underlying catalyst behind the 12-month decline hasn't actually resolved, this "stabilization" is just low-volatility noise inside an intact downtrend — and the superinvestor buyers end up averaging down for another quarter or two rather than having called the bottom.
